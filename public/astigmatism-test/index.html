Astigmatism vs Normal Vision

To understand how astigmatism affects your sight, it is helpful to compare astigmatism vs normal eyes. In a normal eye, the cornea and lens are spherical—curved equally in all directions, like a basketball. This allows light to bend evenly and focus on a single point on the retina.

With astigmatism, the surface of your cornea is oval or football-shaped. This mismatch causes light to focus on multiple points in your eye, resulting in blurry, distorted, or double vision at any distance.

What Causes Astigmatism?

So, what causes astigmatism? It is usually a genetic condition present from birth. It can also develop after an eye injury, surgery, or due to a rare condition called keratoconus, which thins the cornea. Crucially, reading in low light or sitting too close to the TV does not cause or worsen astigmatism.

Why Do Astigmatism Lights Look Blurry at Night?

If you drive at night and notice halo effects or starbursts spreading out from streetlamps and headlights, you are seeing typical astigmatism lights. Because the cornea cannot bend light uniformly, bright points of light stretch out into lines or smears. This headlight glare makes night driving particularly challenging for those with uncorrected refractive errors.

💡 Stigmatism vs Astigmatism: You might hear people refer to this condition as "stigmatism." However, stigmatism vs astigmatism is not a debate between two different disorders. "Stigmatism" is simply a common mispronunciation. The correct medical term is astigmatism (derived from the Greek root "a-", meaning without, and "stigma", meaning a point).

Corrective Options: Glasses vs Toric Contacts

Correcting astigmatism is simple. Most people start with custom prescription astigmatism glasses. These lenses have a cylindrical corrective power that offsets the irregular curve of the cornea.

If you prefer contacts, standard spherical contacts will not work well because they do not stay in one orientation. Instead, you need specialized contact lenses for astigmatism, known as toric lenses. These are weighted at the bottom to prevent them from rotating when you blink.

Can You Wear Colored Contacts for Astigmatism?

Yes! In the past, cosmetic options were limited, but you can now easily buy custom colored contacts for astigmatism. These lenses provide the precise toric correction you need to see clearly while allowing you to change your eye color safely.

Can LASIK Fix Astigmatism Permanently?

If you are looking for a permanent solution, you might wonder: can lasik fix astigmatism? Yes, modern LASIK surgery reshapes the cornea, making it rounder so light focuses directly on the retina. A consultation with an ophthalmologist can confirm if you are a candidate.
